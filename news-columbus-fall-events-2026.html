COMMUNITY • COLUMBUS, OHIO

Free Fall Events Bring Families Across Columbus for a Full Saturday of Activities

By Ryan Lotter | Sinist3rKr3w Media

COLUMBUS, Ohio — Families had several opportunities to enjoy free fall activities across Columbus Saturday, with events stretching from the Linden neighborhood to Downtown and the Short North.

Sinist3rKr3w Media visited three events throughout the day — the Linden Fall Festival, the Free Harvest Fair at Columbus Commons and Hops on High featuring HighBall Halloween — traveling between the events using COTA public transportation.

Linden Fall Festival

The fourth annual Linden Fall Festival was held from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. at Greater Columbus Community House on East 17th Avenue.

Organized through Nationwide Children's Hospital's Healthy Neighborhoods Healthy Families initiative, the free event was designed to bring families together while connecting residents with community resources. Activities advertised by organizers included pumpkin decorating, inflatables, face painting, a photo booth, food trucks, winter clothing giveaways and entertainment.

For families looking for an inexpensive Saturday activity, the event provided multiple activities without requiring an admission fee.

Source: Nationwide Children's Hospital

Harvest Fair at Columbus Commons

From Linden, Sinist3rKr3w Media traveled Downtown to Columbus Commons for the rescheduled Free Harvest Fair.

The noon-to-4 p.m. event featured fall activities including pumpkin decorating, face painting, balloon twisting, a petting zoo and entertainment. Free carousel rides and lawn games were also offered, weather permitting. Food trucks and sweet treats were available separately for purchase.

Families could pick up pumpkins and participate in several activities without paying admission.

Source: Columbus Commons

Hops on High

The final stop was Hops on High featuring HighBall Halloween in the Short North Arts District.

The event transformed part of North High Street into a pedestrian-oriented festival featuring local makers and artists, performances, food and fall activities.

Getting Around Without a Car

The three events also demonstrated that families using public transportation could reach several Columbus activities in a single day.

Sinist3rKr3w Media traveled between Linden, Downtown Columbus and the Short North using COTA buses and walking.

For families watching their budgets, events offering free admission and activities can provide an opportunity to spend a day exploring Columbus without the admission costs associated with many traditional attractions.
