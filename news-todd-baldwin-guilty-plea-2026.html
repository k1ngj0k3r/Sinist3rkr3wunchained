Former California High School Operations Manager Pleads Guilty to Enticing Students for Sexual Abuse Material

By Ryan Lotter | Sinist3rKr3w Media
October 5, 2026
Crime & Courts • California

SAN JOSE, Calif. — A former operations manager at a California high school has pleaded guilty to federal charges after admitting that he paid teenage boys thousands of dollars to produce sexually explicit images and videos.

Todd Baldwin, 46, of Bremerton, Washington, pleaded guilty September 22 in federal court to two counts of online child enticement and two counts of receipt of child pornography, according to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California.

Baldwin also admitted to enticing two additional victims in conduct that was not separately charged and to possessing child sexual abuse material on electronic devices seized from his home, according to federal prosecutors.

According to his plea agreement, Baldwin used internet services and applications including Snapchat, Venmo and Gmail between approximately October 2021 and August 2023 to persuade several minor boys to create sexually explicit material.

Federal prosecutors said Baldwin admitted paying four boys, ages 16 and 17, thousands of dollars through Venmo and Cash App in exchange for images and videos. He told some of the teenagers that he planned to resell the material to buyers online. Prosecutors also said Baldwin solicited clothing worn by boys for resale.

At the time of the offenses, Baldwin worked as an operations manager at Valley Christian High School in San Jose. The victims included students from Valley Christian and Live Oak High School in Morgan Hill. Federal prosecutors said Baldwin became acquainted with at least one victim through his position at the school.

The case was investigated by the FBI and the San Jose Police Department's Internet Crimes Against Children Squad. Baldwin was originally charged by federal complaint in October 2024 and later by an Information filed November 19, 2024.

Baldwin is currently released on bond under conditions that include electronic monitoring and home detention.

His sentencing hearing is scheduled for January 26, 2027, at 8:30 a.m. before U.S. District Judge Beth L. Freeman.

Baldwin faces a mandatory minimum sentence of 10 years in federal prison and a maximum statutory penalty of life imprisonment. He also faces a fine of up to $250,000, special assessments and restitution to his victims. The final sentence will be determined by the court after consideration of federal sentencing law and guidelines.
